360° view and laser pointer

See it the way they see it. Point out what matters.

The Director's preview can now switch from the flat panorama to a 360° view you can look around, just like a viewer in a headset. And a laser pointer puts a glowing red dot in every headset, right where you point.

360° view

Look around, as a viewer would

Press 360° view, or V, and the preview becomes the scene from the inside. Drag to look around and scroll to zoom. Everyone's markers and fields of view follow, and clicking still marks a point to turn everyone towards.

Laser pointer

A red dot in every headset

Switch on the laser, or press L, and point at the preview. A glowing dot with a short trail appears in every headset on that exact spot, wherever each viewer is facing. Click to pin it while you talk.

Both work in the flat and 360° views. Looking around only changes your view on the PC; nobody in a headset is moved unless you choose to turn everyone.

How it works

You run the room from one PC. The headsets follow.

Full Circle Director runs on a Windows PC. Each headset opens Full Circle Player in its browser and joins over your Wi-Fi. There's nothing to install on the headsets.

Step 1

Add your media

Drop 360° videos and photos into the library, singly or as a folder for a guided series. Mono, 3D, 180° and flat films are all supported.

Step 2

Send it to the headsets

Every headset keeps its own copy, so playback never depends on Wi-Fi speed and 8K stays smooth. Do it once, before people arrive.

Step 3

Direct the session

Press play and every headset starts together. Pause, skip and step through photos for everyone at once, see what each person is looking at, and point things out with the laser.

Director → headsets

Small commands, not streamed video

The PC sends a short instruction such as "be at 1:23.4 at this exact moment". Each headset keeps its own clock matched to the PC and nudges its speed to stay in step.

Where it's used

Built for groups who need to see the same thing

Training

Walk a whole team through a real place

Show a site, a ward or a procedure in 360° and talk the group through it together. You can see who has spotted the hazard and who is looking the other way.

  • Health and safety inductions and site walk-throughs
  • Clinical, emergency and first-response scenarios
  • Mock courtrooms and moot practice
  • Onboarding and soft-skills role play
VR performances

Start the show for everyone at once

Run immersive theatre, dance and film screenings like a cinema: everyone is seated, the lights go down and the piece starts on every headset together, with sound in step.

  • Immersive theatre and dance
  • 360° and 3D film screenings and festivals
  • Gallery and museum installations
Education

Take the class somewhere else

Lead a virtual field trip and step through a series of 360° photos with Previous and Next, or a presentation clicker, while you teach.

  • Virtual field trips and heritage sites
  • Science and geography in 360°
  • Careers and workplace tasters
Tours & presentations

Point everyone at what matters

Mark a spot on the preview and turn every viewer to face it, or switch on the laser pointer and a red dot appears in every headset where you point. Then talk them through the detail.

  • Property and venue tours
  • Architecture and design reviews
  • Exhibitions and product launches
Through their eyes

See what every viewer sees, side by side

The Director shows each person's view live, one at a time or all together in a grid. You know at a glance who is with you and who needs a nudge.

Photo series

Step everyone through a set of 360° photos

Put a set of photos in a folder and the Director treats it as a series. Press Next, or a button on a presentation clicker, and every headset moves on together. Try it here with a walk along the beach.

Full Circle Director

Everything you need on one screen

The control panel opens in the browser on the PC and fits a laptop screen, so you can run a session without hunting for anything.

  • Live look map. Every viewer's field of view, drawn on the video as it plays.
  • Turn everyone to a point. Click the picture, press one button, and every headset faces that spot.
  • 360° view. Switch the preview from the flat panorama to the scene as a viewer sees it, and drag to look around.
  • Laser pointer. A glowing red dot in every headset, exactly where you point.
  • Play, pause, skip, stop. For every headset at once, starting together.
  • Headset health. Battery, sync and storage for each headset, with a warning when something needs attention.
  • Library with folders. Add files from the panel, group photos into series and send them to the headsets in one go.

The whole room, in one grid

Open "View all headsets" to watch every viewer's view at once, with their sync and battery on each tile. Play, pause and step through a series without leaving the grid.

Full Circle Player

On the headset, it just plays

Viewers open the Player once, press Enter VR, and the operator does the rest. A waiting screen shows until the first video or photo is loaded.

  • Stored on the headset. Media is copied to each headset in advance, so playback is smooth and never buffers.
  • Sharp 8K. Video is handed straight to the headset's display system, the same route native players use.
  • Mono, 3D, 180° and flat. Top-bottom and side-by-side 3D, VR180, and a virtual cinema screen for ordinary films.
  • No app store. Runs in the headset's browser. Built for Meta Quest 3, and designed for other headsets that support WebXR.

What you need

Kit you probably already have

The director

A Windows laptop or PC

Windows 10 or 11 and 8 GB of memory. A graphics chip that decodes 8K gives a full-picture preview; without one, the panel shows a map of where everyone is looking instead.

The network

A good Wi-Fi router

A Wi-Fi 6 router on the 5 GHz band, with the PC plugged in by cable. No internet connection is needed during a session.

The audience

VR headsets

Meta Quest 3 for the best 8K playback. There's no fixed limit on numbers; a good router handles 20 to 30 headsets comfortably.

Get started

Free to use. No subscription.

Full Circle Director and Full Circle Player cost nothing to use. There are no subscriptions, licence fees or accounts, and everything runs on your own PC and Wi-Fi. It's free for personal, education and business use, including paid training and events. It may not be changed or sold on. Everything you need is on GitHub.

  1. Step 1Download itOn the GitHub page, choose Code → Download ZIP and unzip it on your Windows PC.
  2. Step 2Install Node.jsGet the free LTS version from nodejs.org. It's what runs the Director.
  3. Step 3Start the DirectorDouble-click START.bat, then follow the setup guide that comes with it to connect your headsets.
